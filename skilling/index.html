What it is

Forty-five minutes, and something you can use on Monday

One session, one topic, taught by someone who uses it. About thirty minutes of talk and demo, then ten to fifteen minutes of questions. You can come with the camera off and say nothing at all.

There are two kinds of session, and both are open to everyone. You do not need to be in a mentoring cohort, and you do not need to work at Microsoft.

Skilling sessions

The technical ones. Power Apps, Power Automate, Power BI, Dataverse, Copilot Studio and agents, and whatever else the community is actually building with: GitHub Copilot, security, governance, Azure.

Most sessions are pitched at beginner or intermediate. If a topic assumes something, the announcement says so.

Power Skills

Once a month, the other half of a career: being visible, speaking up in technical rooms, asking for what you are worth, getting your work seen.

Power skills, not soft skills. They draw our largest rooms, which tells you something.

How to get to one

Every session is announced on the Women in Power page on LinkedIn with the joining link. Follow the page and you will not miss one.

Session times rotate so that India, Europe and the Americas each get a workable slot, and every session is recorded. A bad time zone is not a reason to give up on a topic you want.

Why there are no names here yet

Dates, titles and speakers go out on LinkedIn as each speaker confirms. We hold a session back until she is ready for her name to be on it, so this page shows the shape of the season and LinkedIn shows the line-up.

December

A skilling advent calendar

December is a hard month to hold a live session in. So the series swaps them for one door a day, 1 to 24 December, on the Women in Power page on LinkedIn. Open them whenever you like.

Every day of the week has a job, so you learn the rhythm after the first week and know what is coming.

Teach one

You know something worth forty-five minutes

You do not need to be an MVP, and you do not need a new idea. The thing you worked out last month, the thing you now do without thinking: somebody in this community is stuck on exactly that.

Three of the six sessions this season were somebody’s first talk. That is not an accident, it is who we go looking for.

What we ask of you

  • A title, two or three sentences about it, and a short bio
  • Thirty minutes of talk, and ten to fifteen of questions
  • One reply to a check-in two weeks out
  • A reshare of the announcement post, in your own words

What we do for you

  • Set up the Teams event, and send the invite
  • Promote it, but never before you have confirmed
  • Put a moderator in the room to open, introduce and run the chat
  • Offer a dry run if you want one, and record the session so you can keep it

Questions

Before you ask

Is there a fee?

No. The series is free, and it is run by volunteers around their full-time jobs. All it costs you is the forty-five minutes.

Do I have to be in a mentoring cohort?

No. The Skilling Series is open to everyone, whether or not you have ever joined anything else of ours. Plenty of people start here.

The times never work for me.

Times rotate deliberately, so that India, Europe and the Americas each get a workable slot across a season. Every session is recorded, so pick the topic and catch up afterwards.

Do I have to work with Power Platform?

No. Sessions cover Copilot and AI tools, GitHub Copilot, security, governance, Azure and careers as well. If you work near any of this, you will find something.

Are the sessions recorded?

Yes, all of them, and the recording is shared with the community afterwards.

I have never given a talk. Can I still speak?

Yes, and that is the point. More than fifty women have given their first technical talk in this series. The speaker guide has a section written for exactly this, including an outline you can borrow and what to do if your demo breaks.

Can I suggest a topic, or a speaker?

Yes. The call for speakers is the formal route and it opens on 24 December, but you can suggest a topic or nominate somebody any time through the LinkedIn page. The December community day includes a vote on what 2027 should cover.
